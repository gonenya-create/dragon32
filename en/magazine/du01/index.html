When the Dragon got its own magazine: the first issue of Dragon User

My Dragon 32 was a present for my 13th birthday, in 1982. In May 1983 Britain got Dragon User, which billed itself as the country's first monthly magazine devoted entirely to Dragon owners. I lived in Israel, and it never reached me. Now I have read the scan and asked myself what would have caught a 13-year-old's eye. This series goes through the issues one at a time. Here is the first.

What things cost

An advert from Premier Microsystems offered a new Dragon 32 for £199.50 including VAT, plus £3 postage. The Telewriter word processor was £49.95, and the game Donkey King was £8. Dreaming of a disk drive? The news pages said a single-drive system with interface and operating system would cost £275, more than the computer itself.

The factory in Wales

Production had moved to a new factory at Kenfig, near Port Talbot. It was turning out just under 5,000 machines a week, and managing director Tony Clarke talked of reaching 10,000. Another item promised a new machine at about £400, aimed at the BBC Model B.

Head to head

Boris Allan set the Dragon against the ZX Spectrum, the Commodore Vic20 and the BBC. He rated the 6809 as the best 8-bit chip available, but grumbled that in Dragon Basic spaces are sometimes needed and sometimes not.

Tape troubles

The letters page was mostly about cassettes. One reader with a cheap £5 recorder found that saves failed because the motor took too long to get up to speed. His cure was to start the tape by hand just before pressing Enter, and to stop every ten minutes or so to save his work. Advice I could have used.

Where is the British software?

John Symes of Microdeal, in Cornwall, said that nearly all the games he sold were conversions of Tandy originals. He did not spare the computer's maker: “Dragon has been of no help whatsoever to any of the software houses”. He wanted British programmers to start writing their own.

Landing on the moon

For readers who liked typing in listings, David Windle's Moonbase Alpha asked you to land a shuttle on the pad with only 200 units of fuel. And the month's competition offered an Epson MX80F/T III printer.
