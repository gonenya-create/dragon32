Dragon Logo: learning to program with a turtle

Dragon Logo by Larry Kheriaty and George Gerhold (Micropi, 1982) came on a cartridge from Dragon Data. It is the same program the Color Computer sold as Color Logo. You give orders to a small turtle on the screen, and it draws. This is how children learned to program.

Starting

Put the cartridge in with the computer switched off, then switch on. You see LOGO:, and that is the menu: press one letter, without ENTER.

RRun mode: the screen for the turtle, and three lines for commands at the bottom
EThe editor: this is where you write procedures
S, T, ENTERSave all procedures to tape
L, T, ENTERLoad from tape
PPrint on a printer
BREAKFrom any mode: back to LOGO:

First steps

Press R and type one command per line, with a space before the number, then ENTER. FD 40 moves the turtle 40 steps forward, and RT 90 turns it right a quarter turn.

FD / BKForward / back
RT / LTTurn right / left, in degrees
PU / PDPen up / pen down
HOMEBack to the middle, facing up
CLEARClears the screen. Type the word, not the key
HT / STHide / show the turtle
PC 0–3Pen colour
BG 0–3Background colour

A first procedure

A procedure is a new word you teach the turtle. Press BREAK, then E, and type:

TO SQUARE
REPEAT 4 (FD 30 RT 90)
END
TO FLOWER :N
REPEAT :N (SQUARE RT 360/:N)
END

Press BREAK, then R, and type FLOWER 12. The turtle draws 12 squares around the middle, like a flower. :N is a number you hand to the procedure.

What is different from other Logos

  • Round brackets. REPEAT 4 (FD 30 RT 90), not the square brackets most Logos use.
  • REPEAT only inside a procedure. Run mode takes only simple commands, one per line. Loops and conditions go in the editor.
  • A space after every word. FD40 with no space says the turtle does not know that word. Any key carries on.
  • Whole numbers only. No fractions and no lists. Instead you get several turtles at once, and a DOODLE mode for small children: press @ in run mode and draw with the number keys.
  • A circle. REPEAT 36 (FD 6 RT 10) inside a procedure gives a good circle.
  • Keep out of the bottom lines. A turtle that wanders into the three command lines gets smeared when the text scrolls.

First lesson: a square, then a house

A square, one command at a time.

  1. At LOGO: press R. The screen clears, and the turtle stands in the middle, facing up.
  2. Type FD 40 and ENTER: the turtle draws a line upwards. Then RT 90 and ENTER: the turtle turns right.
  3. Repeat the two commands three more times, each on its own line. There is your square, and the turtle is back where it started.

A house, with procedures. REPEAT works only inside a procedure, so the house is written in the editor.

  1. BREAK takes you back to LOGO:, and E opens the editor. Type these lines, each followed by ENTER:
TO SQUARE
REPEAT 4 (FD 30 RT 90)
END
TO ROOF
RT 30
FD 30
RT 120
FD 30
LT 150
END
TO HOUSE
SQUARE
FD 30
ROOF
END
  1. What it does: SQUARE draws the walls. FD 30 takes the turtle up to the top left corner. ROOF draws a triangle for the roof, and at the end turns the turtle back to face up.
  2. Leave the editor with BREAK. There is no special exit command: what you typed is kept as procedures. Press R, type CLEAR and ENTER, then HOUSE and ENTER. The turtle draws a house: a square with a roof on top.
  3. A second house: at the end of HOUSE the turtle stands at the top right corner of the walls. Type, each followed by ENTER: PU, BK 30, RT 90, FD 20, LT 90, PD, and HOUSE again. Now there are two houses side by side. HT hides the turtle.
  4. Saving: put a tape in and press RECORD and PLAY on the recorder. Then BREAK, and S (SAVE: appears), T and ENTER. When LOGO: comes back, your procedures are on tape.
  5. Next time: wind the tape back and press PLAY. At LOGO: press L, T and ENTER. When LOGO: comes back, press R: your procedures are there again.
