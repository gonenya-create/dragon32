Chapter 3

The odometer: counting like the computer

About 20 minutes · no new commands

1Why a different way of writing at all?

You have seen $0400 dozens of times by now, always with a translation next to it: 1024. Time to understand it.

We count with ten digits, 0 to 9. The computer likes to count with 16 signs: 0 to 9, and after them A, B, C, D, E, F. The $ in front says: “this number is written in counting by 16”.

Why is this handy? Because everything fits exactly: a number in one house is always two digits ($00 to $FF), a house number is always four digits, and a row on the screen is exactly $20.

2A car’s odometer

Remember the old odometer in a car? Wheels with digits. The right wheel turns, and when it passes 9 it goes back to 0 and moves the wheel next to it on by one.

The computer’s odometer is exactly the same, except that each wheel goes up to F before it goes back to 0.

3Turn it yourself

The odometer starts at $0400, the first house of the screen. Under it, the same number in ordinary counting. Press the buttons and watch the wheels.

    Look at the +$20 button: it always takes you one row down on the screen. $20 is 32, and a row has 32 squares.

    4What each wheel is worth

    In ordinary counting, in 254 the digit 2 is worth two hundred, because each place to the left is worth 10 times more. On the computer’s odometer each place to the left is worth 16 times more: 1, 16, 256, 4096.

    5The screen map, this time in the computer’s way of writing

    Now the numbers at the side of the screen make sense. Each row starts $20 after the one before. After 8 rows the wheel rolls over, and the 4 becomes 5: halfway down the screen we reach $0500.

    And inside the row? Add the column number, also in the computer’s way of writing. Column 15 is $0F, and column 16 is $10. So the square in row 8, column 16, is $0500 plus $10: $0510.

    From now on, on the screens in the windows, the column numbers along the top are in the computer’s way of writing: 00 to 1F.

    ▶On the real Dragon

    In DREAM you can write numbers both ways. STA $0400 and STA 1024 are exactly the same line: a number without $ counts as ordinary.

    And BASIC has its own sign for the computer’s way of writing: &H. Try:

    PRINT &H0400

    And the computer will answer 1024.

    What we saw on our Dragon machine: in BASIC, PRINT &H0400 answered 1024, and PRINT &H05FF,&HFF answered 1535 and 255. In DREAM, the lines LDA #88 and STA 1056 were translated into exactly the same numbers as LDA #$58 and STA $0420, and the X appeared in square $0420. ✓ Checked on our Dragon machine
    What we learned: $ means “counting by 16”. Each wheel goes 0 to F, then rolls over and moves its neighbour on.
    A row on the screen is $20. The screen is $0400 to $05FF, and the middle is $0500.
